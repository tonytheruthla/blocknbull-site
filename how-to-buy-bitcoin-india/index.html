How to Buy Bitcoin in India (2026): A Complete Beginner's Guide

Short answer: Buying Bitcoin in India is legal in 2026. You need an account on an exchange registered with FIU-IND, completed KYC using your PAN and Aadhaar, and INR deposited via UPI or NEFT. The whole process takes 20–30 minutes for most people, and you can start with as little as ₹100 — you do not need to buy a whole Bitcoin. Profits are taxed at a flat 30% plus cess, with 1% TDS deducted on transfers above ₹10,000 in a financial year.

Is buying Bitcoin legal in India in 2026?

Yes. Buying, selling, and holding Bitcoin is legal in India. It is classified as a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) under Indian tax law, which is precisely why it's taxed — the government taxes it because it recognises it.

What is regulated is the platform you use. Every exchange serving Indian users must register with FIU-IND (the Financial Intelligence Unit) under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act. As of FY 2024–25, 49 exchanges had completed that registration — 45 domestic and 4 offshore.

The enforcement is real. In October 2025, 25 offshore platforms including BitMEX and LBank were banned for non-compliance. Binance, which was effectively blocked through most of 2024, registered with FIU-IND in August 2024 and restored full access for Indian users.

What this means for you: using a registered exchange isn't just safer, it's the difference between a platform that will still exist next year and one that might be blocked.

What are the exact steps to buy Bitcoin in India?

Step 1 — Choose an FIU-registered exchange. Check the official list. Domestic platforms are simpler for pure INR use; global platforms give you better fees and depth if you'll trade actively.

Step 2 — Create your account. Email and phone number. Takes under two minutes.

Step 3 — Complete KYC. This is mandatory before any trading. You'll need your PAN card and Aadhaar. Aadhaar OTP-based eKYC typically completes in 10–15 minutes; document upload with manual review takes 1–2 hours, sometimes a day.

Step 4 — Deposit INR. UPI is fastest — deposits usually reflect within 30 seconds. NEFT and IMPS also work. Most banks cap UPI at around ₹1 lakh per day, though this varies by bank and account type.

Step 5 — Place your order. Choose Bitcoin, enter an amount in rupees, confirm. A market order fills immediately at the current price. A limit order lets you set your price and usually costs a lower fee.

Step 6 — Decide where it lives. Leaving it on the exchange is convenient and means you can trade quickly. Moving it to a personal wallet means you control it — and you're fully responsible for the seed phrase, because there is no password reset in self-custody.

How much money do you need to start?

₹100 on most Indian platforms.

This is the single biggest misconception among Indian beginners. You do not buy "a Bitcoin" — you buy an amount of Bitcoin. It divides into 100 million units, and the smallest is called a satoshi (0.00000001 BTC).

If Bitcoin trades at ₹55,00,000, then ₹100 buys you roughly 0.0000018 BTC. That's a perfectly normal thing to own.

The real barrier is knowledge, not capital. Which is why starting small while you learn is more sensible than waiting until you can afford a large amount.

What tax will you pay on Bitcoin in India?

This is where most Indian beginners get caught out, so understand it before you buy, not after.

Tax Rate Notes
Tax on gains 30% flat + cess Income slab irrelevant. Holding period irrelevant.
TDS on transfers 1% Section 194S, above ₹10,000/year. A prepayment, not extra tax.
Deductions allowed Cost of acquisition only Not fees, not electricity, nothing else.
Loss offset None Section 115BBH — losses offset nothing, carry forward nowhere.

That last row matters more than people realise. If you make ₹50,000 on Bitcoin and lose ₹50,000 on another coin, your net profit is zero — and you still owe roughly ₹15,600 in tax.

The 1% TDS is not an additional tax. It's credited against your final liability when you file, and refundable if you overpaid. Many people never claim it.

Reporting is line-by-line in Schedule VDA within ITR-2 or ITR-3, using FIFO cost basis. Budget 2026 kept both rates unchanged and added penalties: ₹200 per day for missed statements and ₹50,000 for incorrect information.

What mistakes do Indian beginners make most?

Using an unregistered offshore platform. 25 were banned in October 2025. If your funds sit on a platform that gets blocked, recovering them becomes very difficult.

Not knowing the loss rule before buying. People diversify across many volatile altcoins assuming losers will cancel winners at tax time. In India they don't.

Buying with market orders every time. Limit orders usually carry lower fees, and on some exchanges maker orders are free.

Not saving trade records. You'll need every transaction for Schedule VDA. Export your history quarterly — reconstructing a year from memory in July is where errors and penalties come from.

Sending crypto to a wrong address. Bitcoin transactions are irreversible. No support desk can undo one. Always send a small test amount first.

Losing the seed phrase. In self-custody there's no recovery. Write it on paper, store it somewhere safe, never photograph it or put it in cloud storage.
