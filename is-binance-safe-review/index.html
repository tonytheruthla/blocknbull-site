Is Binance Safe? Binance India Review (2026)

Short answer: Yes, with real caveats. Binance is FIU-IND registered and legal to use in India as of 2026, after an early-2024 access block and a ₹188.2 million (~$2.25M) penalty for Prevention of Money Laundering Act violations. Its security track record is strong at the exchange level — no major breach of Binance's own custody since 2019 — but 2026 brought a fresh credential-stuffing incident affecting 420,000 users (attributed to compromised devices, not a platform hack) and a withdrawn EU license application in Greece. Whether that adds up to "safe enough" depends on how much weight you put on regulatory friction versus custody security.

Is Binance legal in India in 2026?

Yes. Binance operates in India as a Registered Reporting Entity with FIU-IND (India's Financial Intelligence Unit), which means it must comply with Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) obligations, including reporting suspicious activity to Indian authorities.

That status followed a rocky patch. In January 2024, FIU-IND ordered Binance and eight other offshore exchanges blocked in India for operating without local registration. Binance paid a ₹188.2 million (~$2.25 million) penalty for PMLA violations and completed FIU-IND registration in August 2024 — restoring website and app access for Indian users, and marking what Binance's own blog called its "19th global regulatory milestone."

One distinction worth understanding: FIU-IND registration is an anti-money-laundering compliance status, not a product approval from SEBI or the RBI. Binance is legally permitted to operate and subject to India's AML reporting rules — it is not bank-regulated, and user funds are not protected the way a bank deposit is.

Has Binance actually been hacked?

Not recently at the core-custody level — but 2026 brought a different kind of incident worth knowing about.

The historical record: - May 2019: Hackers stole roughly 7,000 BTC (~$40 million at the time) from a Binance hot wallet. The SAFU insurance fund fully reimbursed affected users, and no user ultimately lost funds. - October 2022: A cross-chain bridge exploit on BNB Chain resulted in roughly $570 million in losses. This targeted BNB Chain's bridge infrastructure rather than Binance's exchange custody directly, but it's part of the broader Binance ecosystem's security history. - November 2023: Binance reached a $4.3 billion settlement with US authorities (DOJ, FinCEN, OFAC, CFTC) over Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions-compliance failures. This was a legal and compliance settlement, not a theft of user funds — founder Changpeng Zhao stepped down and later pleaded guilty to related charges.

What's new in 2026: a data breach exposed roughly 420,000 user credentials via infostealer malware. Binance has attributed this to compromised individual user devices rather than a breach of its own platform infrastructure — a meaningfully different risk category from a custody hack, but still a real incident affecting real account security.

The honest framing: no attacker has breached Binance's own exchange custody since 2019, which at Binance's scale and profile is a genuine track record. But "hasn't been hacked" and "has no incidents" aren't the same claim — the 2023 settlement and 2026 credential exposure are real marks against the platform, just in different categories (compliance and account security, not custody theft).

How does Binance's security work now?

Binance publicly emphasizes several layers: the SAFU (Secure Asset Fund for Users) insurance fund, regular proof-of-reserves publication, cold storage for the large majority of user funds, mandatory 2FA, anti-phishing codes, and withdrawal address whitelisting.

As with any exchange, treat "cold storage" and "insured" as claims to verify against Binance's current published attestations rather than take at face value — check Binance's live proof-of-reserves page before trusting a specific number. And as with every centralized exchange, no platform — Binance included — offers a funds guarantee comparable to bank deposit insurance. Money on any exchange is structurally an IOU backed by that exchange's solvency and security practices.

Binance's biggest practical edge is coin selection — it lists more assets than almost any other exchange globally. Its biggest practical gap for Indian users is the same one Bybit has: no native INR rail, so onboarding takes an extra P2P step compared to a domestic platform.

Is Binance a good choice for beginners?

It depends on what you're optimizing for. Binance's interface and mobile app are polished and among the more approachable of the global exchanges, and its sheer coin selection means you're unlikely to find an asset you can't trade. But a first-time Indian buyer whose only goal is holding some Bitcoin will still find the P2P INR onboarding step less convenient than a domestic UPI-native exchange. Binance makes more sense once you know you want access to a wide altcoin selection or deeper markets than a domestic platform offers.

What tax applies to Binance trading for Indian users?

The same rules that apply to any crypto trading in India apply on Binance: a flat 30% tax on gains plus applicable cess, 1% TDS on transfers above ₹10,000 in a financial year (credited against final liability, not an extra tax), and no offsetting of crypto losses against any other gains or income (Section 115BBH). Using a foreign exchange doesn't change India's tax treatment — VDA gains are taxed the same regardless of which registered platform you trade on.
