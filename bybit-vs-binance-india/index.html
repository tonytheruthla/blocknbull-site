Bybit vs Binance: Which Is Better for Indian Traders in 2026?

Both Bybit and Binance are legal to use in India in 2026 — both are registered with FIU-IND, and they are two of only four offshore exchanges that are. Neither lets you deposit rupees straight into a spot wallet; both route Indian users through P2P. The real split is this: Binance is marginally cheaper at the base tier because of its BNB discount, while Bybit offers more INR on-ramp routes and deeper maker-side futures pricing. Neither is categorically "better" — it depends on whether you trade spot or derivatives, and how much you value deposit convenience.

Here's the detail that actually decides it.

Are Bybit and Binance both legal in India in 2026?

Yes — and that's a narrower club than most people assume.

Every exchange serving Indian users must register with FIU-IND (the Financial Intelligence Unit) under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act. As of the most recent count, 49 Virtual Digital Asset Service Providers had registered — 45 onshore and only 4 offshore. Bybit and Binance are two of those four. In October 2025, 25 unregistered offshore platforms were banned outright.

Both got there the hard way:

  • Binance was blocked in January 2024, paid a ₹188.2 million (~$2.25M) penalty for PMLA violations, and completed registration in August 2024.
  • Bybit was fined ₹9.27 crore in January 2025, registered in February 2025, and had full access restored for Indian users by September 2025.

What this actually means: FIU-IND registration is an anti-money-laundering compliance status. It is not a SEBI or RBI product approval. Both platforms are legally permitted to operate and must report suspicious activity to Indian authorities — but your funds carry no deposit insurance and no regulator is supervising the products themselves.

If you're comparing against a platform that isn't on the FIU list, that's not a fee decision — it's a "will this platform still be accessible next year" decision.

The honest read: most retail traders sit at VIP 0. At that level Binance's BNB discount is the single biggest lever — it takes spot from 0.10% to roughly 0.075% without any volume requirement. Bybit has no equivalent native-token spot discount.

On futures the picture flips slightly. Bybit's maker side is competitive and Binance's taker side is marginally cheaper (0.05% vs 0.055%). If you're placing limit orders that add liquidity, that difference favours a maker-heavy strategy; if you're market-taking, Binance edges it.

But keep this in proportion. As we covered in our breakdown of what Indian crypto traders actually pay in fees, the exchange fee is usually the smallest of four costs. On ₹10,00,000 of annual volume with ₹1,00,000 of realised gains, exchange fees run about ₹1,000 — against roughly ₹31,200 in tax. Optimising a 0.025% fee gap while ignoring the 30% tax rule is the wrong order of operations.

How do you get INR onto each platform?

Neither supports a direct INR bank deposit into a spot wallet. This is the single biggest practical friction for Indian users on both platforms, and it's worth being clear-eyed about before you sign up.

Bybit: the primary route is Bybit P2P — you buy USDT from verified merchants using UPI, IMPS, PhonePe or bank transfer. Bybit is also reported to support bank transfer and UPI paths beyond pure P2P, giving it slightly more route variety.

Binance: P2P as well, with 0% P2P trading fees. There's no card-based INR purchase route outside P2P.

What this means in practice: on both platforms you're trading with a counterparty, not the exchange, at the deposit step. Use only in-platform escrow, never settle outside the app, and check merchant completion rates before accepting an order. A domestic INR-native exchange like CoinDCX or CoinSwitch removes this step entirely — that convenience is the trade-off against these platforms' liquidity and product depth.

Which has the better security track record?

This is where the two diverge most sharply, and neither comes out clean.

Bybit suffered the largest crypto exchange hack in history on 21 February 2025 — approximately $1.5 billion, attributed by Chainalysis and government investigators to North Korea's Lazarus Group. The attack didn't breach Bybit's own code: malicious JavaScript was injected into the Safe{Wallet} multisig interface Bybit's signers used, so they approved the attacker's transaction believing it was routine. Bybit secured emergency funding, replenished reserves within 72 hours, and no user lost funds — the exchange absorbed the entire loss.

Binance's own exchange custody hasn't been breached since a $40 million hot-wallet theft in 2019, which the SAFU fund fully reimbursed. But its record carries different marks: a $4.3 billion US settlement in November 2023 over Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions-compliance failures (founder Changpeng Zhao stepped down and pleaded guilty), and in 2026 a credential-stuffing incident exposing roughly 420,000 user accounts via infostealer malware — attributed to compromised individual devices rather than Binance's infrastructure.

How to weigh it: Bybit has a proven catastrophic breach and a proven ability to make users whole within three days. Binance has a longer clean custody record but a heavier compliance history and a live account-security problem. Those are genuinely different risk profiles, not one being strictly safer.

On account security specifically, both require full KYC before withdrawal — that's standard, not a red flag. Bybit's post-incident hardening (reduced reliance on third-party web interfaces for multisig, reinforced transaction monitoring, regular proof-of-reserves publication) is worth reading directly on their own attestation pages rather than taking any review's word for it, ours included. The same applies to Binance's SAFU and proof-of-reserves claims.

And the point that applies to both: no centralized exchange offers deposit insurance comparable to a bank. Money on any exchange is structurally an IOU backed by that exchange's solvency.

Which is better for derivatives, and which for spot?

Derivatives: both run deep books. Bybit's derivatives order book is consistently ranked among the top globally and derivatives are its core product rather than a bolt-on. Binance's futures depth is comparable and its taker fee is marginally lower. For most traders the deciding factor isn't the order book — it's whether you want the BNB fee discount or Bybit's maker-side pricing and VIP structure.

Spot and altcoins: Binance wins on breadth. It lists more assets than almost any other exchange globally, so if your strategy involves smaller-cap tokens, you're more likely to find them there. Bybit's spot selection is solid but narrower.

Beginners: neither is ideal as a first platform for someone whose only goal is owning some Bitcoin — the P2P deposit step adds friction a domestic exchange doesn't have. Both make more sense once you want deeper markets or lower fees at volume. If you're starting from zero, our guide to buying Bitcoin in India covers the simpler route first.

What tax do you pay either way?

Identical. The platform doesn't change India's tax treatment.

  • 30% flat tax on gains from Virtual Digital Assets, plus applicable cess — your income slab and holding period are both irrelevant
  • 1% TDS under Section 194S on transfers above ₹10,000 in a financial year (₹50,000 for specified persons). This is a prepayment credited against your final liability, not an extra tax — and it's refundable if you overpaid
  • Only cost of acquisition is deductible. Not exchange fees, not anything else
  • No loss offset. Under Section 115BBH(2)(b), crypto losses can't be set against any income — including other crypto gains — and can't be carried forward

Using an offshore exchange doesn't exempt you, and it doesn't hide the transaction: exchange-side reporting to the tax department is tightening under Budget 2026 changes. Reporting goes in Schedule VDA inside ITR-2 or ITR-3 — never ITR-1 or ITR-4. Our Schedule VDA filing guide walks through the mechanics.

So which should you actually pick?

There isn't a universal answer, but the decision compresses to three questions:

  1. Do you hold or want to hold BNB? If yes, Binance's 25% spot discount is free money at the base tier and probably decides it.
  2. Are you maker-heavy on futures? Bybit's maker-side pricing and VIP ladder suit patient limit-order traders.
  3. Do you want the widest possible altcoin selection? Binance, comfortably.

If none of those apply strongly, the fee difference at retail size is small enough that deposit convenience and your own read on the two security histories should decide it.

One thing that should not decide it: a bonus offer. Both run new-user promotions, and a one-time bonus is worth far less over a year than a fee structure that matches how you actually trade.
