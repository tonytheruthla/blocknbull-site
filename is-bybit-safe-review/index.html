Is Bybit Safe? Bybit India Review (2026)

Short answer: Yes, with real caveats you should know before signing up. Bybit is FIU-IND registered and legal to use in India as of 2026, after a January 2025 suspension and ₹9.27 crore penalty for operating without registration. It also survived the largest crypto exchange hack in history — a $1.5 billion theft in February 2025 attributed to North Korea's Lazarus Group — and replenished all user funds within 72 hours with no customer losses. Security architecture has since been hardened. Whether that's "safe enough" for you depends on how you weigh a proven incident-response track record against a proven breach.

Is Bybit legal in India in 2026?

Yes. Bybit operates in India as a Virtual Digital Asset Service Provider (VDASP) registered with FIU-IND (India's Financial Intelligence Unit) under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act.

That status didn't come easily. In January 2025, FIU-IND fined Bybit ₹9.27 crore (roughly $1 million) and blocked access to its platform for operating in India without registration. Bybit paid the penalty, completed FIU-IND registration in February 2025, and restored full access for Indian users by September 2025.

One distinction worth understanding: FIU-IND registration is an anti-money-laundering compliance requirement, not a SEBI or RBI product approval. It means Bybit is legally permitted to operate and is subject to India's AML reporting obligations. It does not mean crypto trading itself is regulated the way, say, mutual funds are. Trading on Bybit is legal; it is not government-insured or SEBI-supervised the way domestic securities are.

Was Bybit actually hacked?

Yes — the largest crypto exchange hack in history.

On 21 February 2025, attackers stole approximately $1.5 billion in digital assets, mostly Ethereum, from a Bybit multisig cold wallet. The attack didn't exploit Bybit's own code directly: investigators traced it to a compromised developer environment at Safe{Wallet}, the multisig infrastructure Bybit used, where malicious JavaScript was injected into the interface Bybit's own signers used to approve transactions. Signers believed they were approving a routine internal transfer; they were actually authorizing the attacker's transaction. Chainalysis and multiple government investigators attributed the attack to North Korea's Lazarus Group.

What happened next is the part worth weighing alongside the breach itself:

  • Bybit secured emergency bridge funding from firms including Galaxy Digital, FalconX, and Wintermute
  • Reserves were fully replenished within 72 hours
  • No user funds were lost — the exchange, not customers, absorbed the loss
  • Bybit launched a "LazarusBounty" program offering a 10% reward for recovered funds
  • Wallet architecture, transaction monitoring, and third-party audit processes were overhauled afterward

The honest framing: a $1.5 billion breach is a legitimate red flag, not a footnote. It proves the attack surface — third-party multisig tooling — was real and got exploited. What offsets it, partially, is that Bybit's own custody wasn't breached directly, no customer lost a single dollar, and the response was fast and transparent. Judge both facts together, not separately.

How does Bybit's security work now?

Post-incident, Bybit has publicly emphasized: hardened multisig wallet architecture with reduced reliance on third-party web interfaces, independent security audits, reinforced real-time transaction monitoring, and regular proof-of-reserves publication. As with any exchange, "cold storage" and "audited" are claims to verify against the exchange's current published attestations rather than take at face value — check Bybit's latest proof-of-reserves page before trusting a specific number.

No centralized exchange — Bybit included — offers the security guarantee of self-custody. Funds sitting on any exchange are, structurally, an IOU backed by that exchange's solvency and security practices. That's true of every platform in this comparison, not a Bybit-specific weakness.

Is Bybit a good choice for beginners?

It depends what "beginner" means for you. Bybit's spot trading interface is straightforward, but the platform's real depth is in derivatives and futures — products that carry meaningfully higher risk than spot buying and holding. A first-time Indian crypto buyer whose goal is simply to own some Bitcoin is generally better served starting on a domestic INR-native exchange, then considering Bybit later if they want deeper markets or lower fees at volume.

What tax applies to Bybit trading for Indian users?

The same rules that apply to any crypto trading in India apply on Bybit: a flat 30% tax on gains plus applicable cess, 1% TDS on transfers above ₹10,000 in a financial year (credited against final liability, not an extra tax), and no offsetting of crypto losses against any other gains or income (Section 115BBH). Using a foreign exchange doesn't change India's tax treatment — VDA gains are taxed the same regardless of which registered platform you trade on.
