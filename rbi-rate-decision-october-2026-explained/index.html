The RBI Decides on Wednesday: What a Repo Rate Change Does to Your Money (October 2026)

Published 3 October 2026 · India TradFi

On Wednesday at 10am, six people in Mumbai will make the single most consequential money decision in India this quarter. Not for traders. For everyone with a home loan, a fixed deposit, or a savings account.

The Reserve Bank's Monetary Policy Committee meets Monday to Wednesday, and the rate decision lands on 7 October. The repo rate goes in at 5.25%, where it has sat since the August meeting. What makes this one different: some polled economists expect the committee to raise it, which would be the first hike since February 2023, per Business Standard. Others expect a hold. We are not going to guess. What we can do is show you the machine, so whichever way Wednesday goes, you know exactly what it means for your money.

What is the repo rate, in one minute?

The repo rate is the price banks pay to borrow money from the RBI. When that price rises, everything banks do with money gets repriced: the loans they give you, the deposits they take from you, the bonds the government sells.

Think of it as the wholesale price of money. Retail prices, your EMI, your FD rate, your savings rate, are all marked up or down from it.

Why would the RBI raise rates now?

One word: inflation. Retail inflation hit 4.82% in August, the tenth straight month it has climbed and the highest print of the current CPI series, with food inflation running near 6% and rural prices rising faster than urban, per Finnovate's breakdown of the data. Core inflation, the sticky part that strips out food and fuel, has crossed 4% too.

The RBI's own August minutes flagged the risk that these pressures become broader and more persistent. A rate rise is the standard medicine: make money more expensive, cool demand, slow the price spiral. The cost of that medicine is slower growth and pricier loans, which is exactly why the committee argues about it.

If they hike: what changes for you

Home loan EMIs rise. Most floating-rate home loans in India are repo-linked by regulation, so a 25bp hike flows into your rate within a reset cycle or two. On a ₹50 lakh loan over 20 years, a quarter-point move changes the EMI by roughly ₹800 a month. Small per month, large over two decades.

FD rates improve, with a lag. Banks raise deposit rates after loan rates, and by less. Savers finally get paid more, but patience is required, and the best new FD rates usually appear weeks after the decision.

Bonds fall, yields rise. Existing bonds reprice downward when new money earns more. Debt mutual fund NAVs feel this on day one.

Equity does the maths again. Higher rates make future profits worth less on paper and make borrowing costlier for companies. Rate-sensitive sectors feel it first. That is a description of the machine, not a call on any stock.

If they hold: the quieter signal

A hold with the current inflation picture would tell you the committee believes the price pressure is temporary, or that growth is too fragile to tax with costlier money. Markets read the language of the statement as closely as the number itself. "Neutral" versus "withdrawal of accommodation" is central-bank code for what comes next, and the press conference at 10am often moves markets more than the decision.

The context Wednesday lands in

The Nifty closed Thursday at 22,421.95, down 0.88% on continued foreign selling, and the market was shut Friday for Gandhi Jayanti. So Monday's open is the first trade of RBI week, with three days of positioning before the announcement. Expect the financial channels to talk about little else.

For crypto readers: rate decisions move crypto too, through the same channel as everything else, the price of money. Costlier rupees and higher bond yields compete with every risk asset for the same savings.

The one-line takeaway

You cannot control what six people decide on Wednesday. You can know your own exposure before they decide it: check whether your home loan is repo-linked, know when your FD matures, and remember that the statement's language matters as much as the number.
