Your 10% Bitcoin Bet Can Quietly Become 22%. Portfolio Rebalancing, Explained (October 2026)

Short answer: Rebalancing means bringing your portfolio back to the mix you chose, on a schedule or when it drifts too far. Its job is to control risk, not to boost returns. In our worked example, a 10% bitcoin slice bought in January 2024 grew to 22.5% of the portfolio by bitcoin's October 2025 record without the owner deciding anything. A once-a-year rebalance kept it near 12%. In India, selling crypto to rebalance costs 30% tax plus cess on the gain, so the cheapest way to rebalance is with new money.

What is portfolio rebalancing?

You pick a mix: say 90% in something steady and 10% in bitcoin. Then prices move. The part that rises fastest takes up more of your money, and the part that lags takes up less. After a year or two, your portfolio no longer matches the mix you chose. That gap is called drift.

Rebalancing is the act of moving back. You trim what grew too big and top up what shrank, until the mix matches your plan again.

Vanguard, which manages trillions of dollars in index funds, puts the purpose plainly in its 2015 research paper on the subject: the primary goal "is to minimize risk relative to a target asset allocation, rather than to maximize returns."

How does a 10% bitcoin bet turn into 22%?

Here is a worked example using real bitcoin prices from Kraken. It is hypothetical: one portfolio, one path, no fees. To keep the maths clean, the other 90% earns a steady 7% a year, like a fixed deposit, and rupee moves are ignored.

On 4 January 2024 you put ₹1,00,000 to work: ₹90,000 in the steady part and ₹10,000 in bitcoin at $42,850.

Bitcoin then ran to a record $126,198 on 6 October 2025, almost three times your entry. If you never touched the portfolio, your ₹10,000 slice was worth about ₹29,450, and bitcoin made up 22.5% of everything you owned. You chose 10%. The market chose 22.5% for you.

Then bitcoin fell. By the week of 25 June 2026 it touched $57,740, more than 54% below its record. The bigger slice took the bigger hit.

Did rebalancing make more money?

In this example, yes, slightly, and it hurt less on the way down.

Highest bitcoin share along the way 22.5% 12.4%

Do not read too much into the return gap. That depended on this one path: bitcoin rose, then fell. In a market that only goes up, the never-rebalanced portfolio usually wins on return, because it keeps more of the asset that keeps rising.

Vanguard's long-run numbers show exactly that trade-off. It tested a 50% stock and 50% bond portfolio from 1926 to 2014. Left alone, the stock share drifted to 97% at its peak and averaged 81%. The never-rebalanced version earned a higher average return, 8.9% a year against 8.1%, but with far more risk: volatility of 13.2% against 9.9%. It was no longer a 50/50 portfolio. It had quietly become a stock portfolio.

That is the point. Rebalancing does not promise more money. It promises that the risk you are carrying is the risk you chose.

How often should you rebalance?

Less often than you think. Vanguard found "no optimal frequency or threshold": monthly, quarterly and yearly schedules produced very similar risk-adjusted results, but monthly rebalancing created far more trades and costs. Its conclusion for most broadly diversified portfolios was to check once or twice a year and rebalance when a holding drifts about 5 percentage points from its target.

For a small crypto slice inside a bigger portfolio, a simple version works:

  1. Write down your target mix. For example, 90% steady, 10% crypto.
  2. Check on a fixed date, once or twice a year. Put it in your calendar.
  3. Act only if the drift is big enough. If 10% has become 15% or more, or 5% or less, bring it back.

Picking the date in advance matters. It takes the decision away from the moment when bitcoin is making headlines and you least want to sell, or when it is crashing and you least want to buy.

What does rebalancing cost in India?

This is where India is different, and it changes the method.

Crypto gains in India are taxed at a flat 30%, plus 4% cess, under Section 115BBH, and a 1% TDS applies on transfers under Section 194S. Losses cannot be set off against other income.

In our example, the January 2025 trim sold about ₹10,195 of bitcoin. The gain on that portion was about ₹5,566, so the tax was roughly ₹1,737, plus about ₹102 of TDS deducted upfront (claimable against your final tax). That tax bill ate most of the ₹2,481 lead the rebalanced portfolio has today. Selling to rebalance is not free.

So the order of preference for an Indian investor is:

  1. Rebalance with new money first. If crypto has grown too big, send your next monthly investments to the other part of your portfolio until the mix is back. No sale, no tax.
  2. Use income. Interest and dividends can go to whatever is underweight. Vanguard found that simply redirecting income captured most of the risk-control benefit with no selling at all.
  3. Sell only when the drift is large, and only enough to bring it back near the target, not necessarily all the way.

What should you actually do with this?

Write down your target mix, pick one date a year, and decide now how far you will let it drift. That is the whole habit. It will not tell you what to buy or when bitcoin will move. It makes sure that when it does, the size of the bet is one you chose, not one the market chose for you.

Frequently asked questions

Is rebalancing the same as timing the market? No. Timing the market means guessing where prices go next. Rebalancing ignores forecasts and only looks at your own mix against your own target, on a schedule you set in advance.

Should I rebalance when bitcoin falls a lot? Only if your mix has drifted past the limit you set. A big fall can push a crypto slice below target, and the rule would then say top it up. That only makes sense inside an allocation you already decided you can live with, and only with money you can afford to lose.

What if my crypto slice keeps going up? Then the never-rebalanced portfolio will likely end with more money, with much more risk. Rebalancing is a choice to give up some upside in exchange for keeping the risk you signed up for.

Does the 30% tax mean I should never sell crypto to rebalance? Not never, but last. Rebalancing with new money or income avoids the tax completely. Sell only when the drift is large enough that the extra risk matters more to you than the tax bill.

The one-line takeaway

Choose your mix once, check it once a year, and use new money to fix the drift before you sell anything.
