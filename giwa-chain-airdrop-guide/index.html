Is There a GIWA Chain Airdrop? What We Actually Know (2026)

No — Dunamu, the operator of Upbit, has not announced a GIWA token or airdrop as of this writing. GIWA is a public testnet Ethereum layer-2 built on the OP Stack in partnership with the Optimism Foundation, and testnet participation is a speculative positioning move based on precedent, not a confirmed reward.

What is GIWA Chain?

GIWA is an Ethereum layer-2 blockchain being built by Dunamu — the company behind Upbit, South Korea's largest cryptocurrency exchange — in partnership with the Optimism Foundation. The partnership, announced 4 May 2026 at Consensus Miami, makes GIWA the first deployment on Optimism's "Self-Managed" tier within its OP Enterprise framework: Upbit keeps control of the primary sequencer and core network decisions, while Optimism contributes technical support and runs a backup sequencer.

Technically, GIWA runs on the OP Stack (the same base framework as Optimism, Base and a growing list of "Superchain" L2s), is fully EVM-compatible, and targets roughly one-second block times.

Has Dunamu confirmed a GIWA token or airdrop?

No. As of this writing, Dunamu has not announced a GIWA token, a token generation event, or any airdrop program. Anything you read framing a GIWA airdrop as confirmed is getting ahead of the actual announcement — treat it as unconfirmed until Dunamu or the Optimism Foundation says otherwise directly.

Why do people expect an airdrop anyway?

Three things create reasonable — not guaranteed — speculation:

  1. Dunamu's scale. Upbit is South Korea's dominant exchange, and a company of that size building dedicated L2 infrastructure signals a serious, well-funded, long-term project rather than a side experiment.
  2. OP Stack's retroactive-reward precedent. Optimism's own OP token had a retroactive airdrop to early users. Other OP Stack chains (including Base-ecosystem projects) have leaned on similar retroactive-reward playbooks. That history is why testnet users on a new OP Stack chain often position early — but precedent from other chains is not a promise from this one.
  3. An open public testnet. GIWA's testnet is live and has processed a large volume of transactions (reported near 100 million at the time of the partnership announcement), which gives users a genuine, no-cost way to interact with the chain now, ahead of any mainnet or token decision.

What does testnet participation actually cost?

Nothing in capital. GIWA's testnet runs on test tokens from an official faucet, not real ETH or real money. The cost is your time: completing on-chain interactions like wallet transactions, contract deployments, or bridging test assets across the network, the same category of activity that other OP Stack testnets have historically rewarded when a token did eventually launch.

Should you spend time on GIWA testnet tasks?

That's a genuinely personal call, not something we'll tell you the "right" answer to. What we can say factually: it costs no capital, only time and a small amount of effort with a faucet and a wallet, and it sits on real, credible infrastructure (Dunamu + Optimism Foundation) rather than an anonymous team. Against that: there is no token, no confirmed airdrop, and no timeline — you could spend meaningful time on testnet tasks and see nothing materialize, exactly as has happened with plenty of other testnets that never converted a following into a token.

If you do participate, use only official channels: GIWA's own testnet faucet and documentation, never a third-party site or a link from a social media ad promising to "speed up" your eligibility — that pattern is exactly how airdrop-adjacent phishing scams operate across this category of project.
