Crypto Tax India 2026: How to File Schedule VDA (Even If You Missed July 31)

Crypto gains in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus 4% cess under Section 115BBH, reported in a dedicated Schedule VDA inside either ITR-2 or ITR-3 — never ITR-1 or ITR-4. If you're a pure investor, that filing deadline was 31 July 2026, and it already passed. You're not out of options, but the clock and the penalties both work differently from here. Here's exactly where things stand.

What actually counts as a Virtual Digital Asset?

Under Section 2(47A) of the Income Tax Act, a VDA is any code, number, or token — not Indian or foreign currency — generated through cryptographic means that represents value and can be transferred, stored, or traded electronically. In practice that means Bitcoin, Ethereum, and every other cryptocurrency, plus NFTs and anything else the government formally notifies.

What's explicitly excluded: the rupee and foreign currencies, the RBI's own Central Bank Digital Currency, and ordinary gift cards, vouchers, or loyalty points. Crypto is legal to buy, sell, and hold in India — it just isn't legal tender, and it comes with its own tax regime rather than falling under the rules for stocks or mutual funds.

How much tax do you actually pay on crypto in India?

It depends on how the crypto reached you or left you — there are four distinct treatments, and mixing them up is the single most common filing mistake.

Category What triggers it Rate Where it's reported
Capital gains Selling, swapping, or spending crypto 30% flat + 4% cess (~31.2% effective) Schedule VDA
TDS (withholding) Every transfer above the threshold 1%, credited against final tax Deducted at source, claimed as credit
Income from other sources Airdrops, mining rewards, staking rewards, crypto received as a gift over ₹50,000, or as payment for casual services Your normal income slab rate Schedule OS
Business income (PGBP) Crypto F&O trading, mining as a business, DeFi operations, freelance/consultancy income paid in crypto Slab rate, computed as business profit Schedule BP, via ITR-3

The capital-gains rate is the one everyone knows, and it's brutal by design: only your original cost of acquisition is deductible — no exchange fees, no other expenses — and you cannot offset a loss on one coin against a gain on another, or against any other income, and you cannot carry a crypto loss forward to next year. That's Section 115BBH(2)(b), and it's unusually strict even by Indian tax standards.

The 1% TDS under Section 194S is the part people most often misunderstand: it is not an additional tax. It's withheld automatically at the point of transaction and credited against whatever you actually owe when you file — refundable if you overpaid. The threshold is ₹50,000 a year for "specified persons" (individuals or HUFs with business turnover under ₹1 crore or professional receipts under ₹50 lakh), and ₹10,000 a year for everyone else.

What is Schedule VDA, and which ITR form do you actually use?

Schedule VDA is the dedicated section — inside ITR-2 or ITR-3 — where every VDA transaction gets reported: date of acquisition, date of transfer, cost of acquisition, and sale consideration. The portal calculates your profit or loss automatically once you enter those four fields, and you can either list every transaction individually or aggregate by asset and holding period.

The form you use depends on how you engage with crypto, not how much you made:

ITR-2 ITR-3
Who it's for Investors — buy/sell as an individual, no business setup Freelancers, business/professional crypto income
Covers Capital gains, staking/airdrop/gift income with no business angle Everything ITR-2 covers, plus crypto F&O, mining-as-business, DeFi operations, consultancy paid in crypto
Deadline (AY 2026-27) 31 July 2026 — passed 31 August 2026 (non-audit) / 31 October 2026 (audit cases)

ITR-1 and ITR-4 cannot report crypto income under any circumstances — if you have VDA activity, you're automatically excluded from both, regardless of how simple your finances otherwise are.

You're reading this in August. Did you already miss the deadline?

If you're an ITR-2 filer — the category most individual crypto investors fall into — yes: 31 July 2026 has passed, and unlike last year, the Income Tax Department has not extended it. Here's what that actually costs you, and what to do next.

If you have unpaid tax: Section 234A adds 1% monthly interest on the outstanding amount, calculated from 1 August until the day you actually file. Section 234F adds a late fee on top — up to ₹5,000, reduced to ₹1,000 if your total income is ₹5 lakh or less. If no tax is actually owed (say, TDS already covered your full liability), the 234A interest doesn't apply — only the 234F fee does.

What you can still do: file a belated return any time before 31 December 2026. It's a real, valid ITR — just with the fee and interest above attached, and with one real cost: any losses you'd otherwise be entitled to carry forward (on non-VDA capital assets — VDA losses were never carry-forward eligible anyway) are forfeited if filed late.

If you're past 31 December next year too: your only route becomes an Updated Return (ITR-U) under Section 139(8A), filable within 24 months of the relevant assessment year, with additional penalties layered on top. It exists specifically so a missed or incorrect filing doesn't become permanent — but it's the expensive, last-resort path, not a substitute for filing on time.

If you're an ITR-3 filer — business or professional crypto income — you still have until 31 August 2026 for non-audit cases. That's roughly three weeks from today. Use them.

What changed in Budget 2026 that most existing guides haven't caught up on?

The headline is what didn't change: the 30% rate and 1% TDS survived Budget 2026-27 untouched, despite industry lobbying. CoinSwitch co-founder Ashish Singhal publicly pushed for cutting TDS to 0.01% and raising the threshold to ₹5 lakh to ease liquidity pressure — the government didn't move on either.

What did change is easy to miss because it doesn't touch individual tax rates directly: starting 1 April 2026, exchanges and other "reporting entities" face real penalties under a new Section 509/446 framework for how they report your transactions to the tax department — ₹200 per day for not filing the required statement, and a flat ₹50,000 if the data they file is inaccurate and not corrected.

That's not a fine on you. But it means the Annual Information Statement (AIS) the tax department holds on your crypto activity is about to get meaningfully more complete and more accurate than it's ever been, because exchanges now have a real financial reason to get it right. Reconcile your Schedule VDA entries against your AIS on incometax.gov.in before you file — a mismatch that used to slip through quietly is far more likely to trigger a notice now.

How do you actually fill out Schedule VDA, step by step?

  1. Log into the e-filing portal at incometax.gov.in with your PAN and password.
  2. Go to e-File → Income Tax Returns → File Income Tax Return.
  3. Select Assessment Year 2026-27, mode Online, status Individual.
  4. Choose ITR-2 or ITR-3 based on the table above.
  5. Under Select Schedule, tick Schedule VDA.
  6. For each transaction (or aggregated entry), enter acquisition date, transfer date, cost of acquisition, and sale consideration. The portal computes the gain or loss automatically.
  7. If you also have airdrop, staking, mining, or gift income, add it separately under Schedule Income from Other Sources — this is a different line from Schedule VDA and taxed at your slab rate, not 30%.
  8. Review, confirm, and e-verify — via Aadhaar OTP, EVC, net banking, or a physical ITR-V. An unverified return is treated the same as one never filed.

Keep exchange-exported transaction CSVs and wallet records on hand before you start — reconstructing cost-of-acquisition data after the fact, across multiple exchanges and years, is the single biggest reason crypto tax filing takes people far longer than they expect.

What happens if you don't report your crypto at all?

Leaving Schedule VDA blank when you have reportable activity doesn't make the return valid with an omission — it can get your entire return marked defective, which in practice is treated close to not having filed at all until you correct and resubmit it. Beyond that: scrutiny notices, reassessment, and in serious or repeated cases, prosecution for tax evasion are all live possibilities, especially now that exchange-side reporting is tightening under the Budget 2026 changes above.

If you've already under-reported in a prior year, an Updated Return (ITR-U) under Section 139(8A) lets you fix it within 24 months of the relevant assessment year — with additional penalties, but before it becomes a bigger problem.

Frequently asked questions

Do I owe crypto tax if I only bought and held, never sold? No. Tax triggers on transfer — selling, swapping, or spending. Buying and holding creates no taxable event, though you should still keep records of your acquisition cost for whenever you eventually do transfer.

Can I offset a crypto loss against my stock market gains? No. Section 115BBH(2)(b) blocks VDA losses from offsetting any other income, and blocks carrying them forward — this is stricter than the rules for equities or mutual funds.

Is the 1% TDS the same as my final tax bill? No. It's a prepayment withheld at the point of transaction and credited against whatever you owe when you file. If your actual liability is lower, you get the difference back; it isn't a separate 1% tax on top of the 30%.

Which ITR form do I use if I only trade occasionally alongside a salaried job? ITR-2, as long as you have no business or professional crypto income. ITR-1 and ITR-4 cannot report VDA income at all, regardless of your other income sources.

I missed the 31 July deadline — is my only option to wait until next year? No. File a belated return any time before 31 December 2026, with the Section 234F late fee (and Section 234A interest, if tax is actually owed) attached. It's a real, valid filing — just a more expensive one than filing on time would have been.

Does the new Budget 2026 penalty apply to me as an individual investor? Not directly — it targets exchanges and reporting entities, not individual taxpayers. Its practical effect on you is that the data the tax department already has about your transactions is becoming more complete, making mismatches between your filing and your AIS more likely to be caught.

Do airdrops and staking rewards get taxed the same as trading profits? No. They're taxed as income from other sources at your slab rate when received, not at the flat 30% VDA rate. If you later sell what you received, that sale is a separate, second taxable event under the 30% capital-gains rule.
