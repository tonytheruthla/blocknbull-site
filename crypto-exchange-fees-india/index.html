Crypto Exchange Fees in India (2026): What You Actually Pay, Compared

Update, 28 October 2026: WEEX and Blofin are among the 15 exchanges FIU-IND ordered blocked in India on 9 September 2026. Their figures below no longer apply to Indian users. See the full blocked list.

Short answer: Indian crypto traders pay four separate costs, not one. Spot trading fees run about 0.075%–0.10% on major global exchanges. Futures fees run roughly 0.02% maker and 0.055% taker. On top of that, India applies a 1% TDS on transfers above ₹10,000 in a financial year and a flat 30% tax on gains. Since July 2025, some exchanges also add 18% GST on trading fees for Indian users. For most Indian traders the exchange fee is the smallest of these four costs — which is exactly why most people optimise the wrong one.

Why do fee comparisons mislead Indian traders?

Because the headline trading fee is rarely the biggest number on your statement.

Run the maths on a trader doing ₹10,00,000 of volume across a year with ₹1,00,000 of realised gains:

Cost Amount
Exchange fees at 0.10% ~₹1,000
1% TDS on transfers Up to ₹10,000 (recoverable as credit)
30% tax on gains + 4% cess ~₹31,200
Total tax and fee burden ~₹32,200

The exchange fee is roughly 3% of your total cost. The tax is roughly 97%.

Switching from a 0.10% exchange to a 0.075% exchange saves you ₹250 on that volume. Understanding that crypto losses can't be offset against gains in India — Section 115BBH — could save you multiples of that in how you structure positions.

Optimise the tax first. Then optimise the fee.

What is the 1% TDS and is it an extra cost?

No — and this is the single most misunderstood cost in Indian crypto.

The 1% TDS under Section 194S is a prepayment of tax, not an additional tax. It applies to transfers above ₹10,000 in a financial year (₹50,000 for specified individuals and businesses), and it's credited against your final liability when you file your return. If you over-paid across the year, you claim the difference back as a refund.

Where it does hurt is working capital. TDS applies to every transfer, not every profit. A high-frequency trader has 1% continuously drained from circulating funds — including on trades that lost money. That's a genuine structural drag on active trading strategies in India, and a real reason many Indian traders reduce their trade frequency rather than chase a 0.025% fee difference.

Does GST apply to crypto trading fees in India?

Since July 2025, some exchanges apply 18% GST on trading fees for Indian users.

Note what's being taxed: the GST is on the fee, not on your trade value or your gains. If you pay ₹1,000 in trading fees, GST adds roughly ₹180. It raises your effective trading cost by about a fifth, but it's applied to the smallest of the four cost buckets.

GST treatment varies between exchanges and has changed more than once — confirm current treatment with your specific exchange.

Which exchange is cheapest for Indian traders?

It depends entirely on how you trade — and any article that gives you a single answer is guessing.

If you trade derivatives: Bybit's 0.020% maker / 0.055% taker on futures is competitive, with meaningful reductions at VIP tiers. Get started on Bybit — new users can claim up to $30,000 in deposit rewards.

If you trade high spot volume: Binance's 0.075% flat rate across maker and taker is the lowest standard spot fee among the majors.

If you mostly place limit orders: WEEX's zero maker fee on limit orders is a genuine structural advantage for patient traders.

If you want INR-denominated simplicity: Domestic Indian exchanges handle INR rails natively, which reduces conversion friction — usually at the cost of higher headline fees and thinner liquidity.

How do you actually reduce your costs?

Five things that move the needle more than exchange-hopping:

  1. Use limit orders instead of market orders. On most exchanges maker fees are lower than taker fees, and on WEEX limit orders are free. This is the single biggest controllable saving for a retail trader.
  2. Claim your TDS credits. That 1% has been accumulating in your name all year. It's already paid. Plenty of people simply forget to claim it and effectively donate it to the government.
  3. Trade less frequently. Every round trip costs fees plus TDS plus spread. In a 30%-flat-tax regime with no loss offset, high frequency is structurally expensive.
  4. Understand the loss rule before you size positions. Crypto losses in India offset nothing and carry forward nowhere. The cost of being wrong is higher here than in equity.
  5. Check whether you qualify for a fee tier. Most exchanges reduce fees by volume or by holding their native token. If you're trading meaningfully, you may already qualify.

Frequently asked questions

What is the lowest fee crypto exchange for Indian users in 2026? For limit orders, WEEX charges 0% maker fees, which is the lowest available structure. For flat spot trading, Binance's 0.075% is the lowest standard rate among major exchanges. For futures, Bybit's 0.020% maker fee is competitive. The best choice depends on your order type and volume.

Is the 1% TDS an extra tax on crypto in India? No. The 1% TDS under Section 194S is a prepayment credited against your final tax liability when you file. If you overpaid, you claim it back as a refund. It affects working capital, not your total tax owed.

Do Indian traders pay GST on crypto trading fees? Since July 2025, some exchanges apply 18% GST on trading fees for Indian users. The GST applies to the fee itself, not to your trade value or gains. Confirm current treatment with your specific exchange.

How much tax do I pay on crypto gains in India? A flat 30% on all gains from Virtual Digital Assets, plus applicable cess and surcharge. Your income tax slab does not matter, holding period does not matter, and only your cost of acquisition is deductible.

Can I offset crypto losses against crypto gains in India? No. Under Section 115BBH(2)(b), crypto losses cannot be offset against any income — including other crypto gains — and cannot be carried forward to future years.

Are deposit fees free for INR on crypto exchanges? INR deposits are typically free across exchanges, though the deposit method, withdrawal fees, and conversion spreads vary. Check the specific rails your exchange supports.
