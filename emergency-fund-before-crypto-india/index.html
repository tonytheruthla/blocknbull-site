Emergency Fund First: How Much to Save Before Crypto (2026)

Published 28 October 2026

Short answer: Before you put money into crypto, keep three to six months of essential expenses somewhere safe and easy to reach, such as a savings account or a liquid fund. If you spend ₹50,000 a month on essentials, that is ₹1.5 lakh to ₹3 lakh. Crypto can fall a third or more in a year, and if you have no cushion, a job loss or a hospital bill can force you to sell at the bottom.

What is an emergency fund?

An emergency fund is money you keep aside only for shocks: losing your job, a medical bill, an urgent family expense. It is not an investment. Its job is to be there, in full, on the day you need it.

That is why it should not sit in anything whose price moves a lot. Money you might need next week cannot be in an asset that can drop 20% by next week.

How many Indians actually have one?

Fewer than you would think. In a September 2026 survey by Finnovate Financial Services, 46% of respondents said they had less than three months of expenses set aside, or no emergency fund at all.

A separate report from Grant Thornton Bharat, published on 27 October 2026, found that 83% of respondents said their savings would last less than a year if their income stopped. That survey covered 528 people, so treat it as a signal, not a census.

How much should you save?

Start with your essential monthly spending: rent or EMI, food, bills, insurance, school fees. Not your total spending, just what you cannot skip.

Your monthly essentials 3 months 6 months
₹25,000 ₹75,000 ₹1,50,000
₹50,000 ₹1,50,000 ₹3,00,000
₹1,00,000 ₹3,00,000 ₹6,00,000

Three months is the floor. Aim for six if you are the only earner, if your income is irregular (freelance, commission, business), or if you support parents or children. Grant Thornton found 74% of respondents support at least one family member.

Why does this matter before crypto?

Because crypto is the money you can afford to see fall, and an emergency fund is what makes that true.

Bitcoin hit a record of about $126,198 on 6 October 2025. On 28 October 2026 it trades near $80,824, about 36% below that record. Nobody who bought near the top did anything wrong by holding. The problem starts when life forces a sale: if you lose your job during a drop like that and your only savings are in crypto, you sell at the bottom and lock the loss in.

Borrowing makes it worse. On 24 October 2026, exchanges force-closed the bets of 196,033 traders in a single day, per CoinGlass data, most of them bets that the price would rise. An emergency fund will not stop a market fall, but it means a fall never decides for you.

Where should you keep it?

Somewhere safe, boring and quick to reach:

  1. Savings account. Instant access. Keep at least one month of expenses here.
  2. Liquid or overnight mutual funds. Low-risk debt funds. Under SEBI rules, many offer instant redemption of up to ₹50,000 or 90% of your holding a day, per scheme, whichever is lower. Larger amounts arrive in a working day or so.
  3. A short fixed deposit with premature withdrawal allowed. The RBI raised its repo rate to 5.50% on 7 October 2026, so check what your bank pays now. Know the penalty for breaking it early.

Not in crypto, not in stocks, and not lent to a friend.

How do you build one from zero?

Set a monthly transfer on payday, before you spend anything else. Even ₹5,000 a month builds ₹60,000 in a year. Put bonuses and windfalls straight into it until you hit three months. Once it is full, stop topping it up and send that money to your investments instead.

Frequently asked questions

Should I stop investing in crypto until my emergency fund is full? If you have no cushion at all, build at least one to three months first. After that, many people split their monthly savings between the fund and investments until the fund reaches its target.

Can I keep my emergency fund in stablecoins? No. Stablecoins can lose their peg, sit on exchanges that can freeze withdrawals, and in India any sale is taxed. An emergency fund belongs in rupees, in a bank or a regulated fund.

Is a credit card a backup emergency fund? Only for a few days. Card interest in India often runs above 40% a year, so a card is a bridge, not a fund.

Do I need an emergency fund if I have health insurance? Yes. Insurance covers hospital bills. It does not pay your rent if you lose your job.

The one-line takeaway

Save three to six months of essentials in a savings account or liquid fund first, then invest only what is left over.
